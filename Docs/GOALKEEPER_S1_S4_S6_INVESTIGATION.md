# ScenarioBench findings S1, S4, S6 — investigation (Phase 29)

Method: traces of the real Core (real `MatchController`, `BasicDecisionDriver`, the bench's own scenes) with an external, read-only harness that calls `GoalkeeperDistribution.Choose` on the same view the keeper sees; every trace was cross-checked against the bench row of the same run. Matched pairs: same seed, same starting positions, only `DistributionEnabled` changes. Seeds 1..40 (the seed moves the receiver sideways by a fixed amount and seeds the controller's noise; the AI is deterministic, so the 40 runs are not 40 independent samples). Production code was not changed. Classic, teams 3 vs 8, keeper `gk-emiliano-martinez`.

## S1 — free receiver: CONFIRMED CAUSE, not a defect of the distribution
- Trace (seed 1, on): frame 0 `Choose` = Short to `cb-cristian-romero`, chance 0.88 (>= the 0.75 release threshold). The keeper's first decision is at 0.28 s in BOTH configurations: the driver makes him "notice" the change of possession after his perception delay (0.16 s, `DecisionProfile.PerceptionDelaySeconds`, `BasicDecisionDriver.HasNoticed`) and decides every 9 steps (0.15 s, `IsDue`); the first due frame (0.13 s) is before he has noticed, the next (0.28 s) plays the pass. Nothing of this is the distribution.
- 40/40 seeds: same release instant (0.283 s), same receiver, on and off. 0/40 seeds (on and off) have a single wait decision before the first release.
- The "~16 decisions" were wait FRAMES (not decisions) counted over the whole run: in seed 1 the ball comes back to the keeper at 1.80 s, no receiver is in range (the team-mate is within 3 m, `PassMinDistance`) and he waits again, as designed, until a lane opens. The column is fixed (`waitSecondsBeforeRelease`, first possession only).
- Recognition of a safe pass: yes (chance 0.88 from the first frame). No evidence of an inability to recognise it.

## S4 — rival at 7 m: CAUSE CONFIRMED (mechanism); one part pending
Correction: the goals are goals AGAINST the keeper's team (`goalBy = away`, 17/40 on, 1/40 off; goals for Home: 0/80). The previous report said the clearance "reaches the far goal": wrong.
- Matched pairs (40 seeds): goal against only with the distribution on 16, only off 0, both 1. With these positions and this Away AI the effect of switching the distribution on is causal in this scene (paired, same inputs), not just 17 vs 1.
- Mechanism (trace seeds 1, 4, 6, 8, 2 and the 40-seed table):
  1. Targetless release is `KeeperKick(view, me, -1)`: `d = LongPassDistance` (14 m), power = `PassPhysics.LoftedPower(14 x LandingShare 0.9, quality)`: the weakest long kick (intensity 0.05, ball (9.36, 5.15 up) m/s, lands about 12 m away). It is a short lob, not a clearance up the pitch. This code is from Phase 28, before Stage 1; Stage 1 uses it as its "Clear".
  2. With the distribution OFF the keeper kicks at 0.28 s with the rival at 6.1 m on average; the rival touches the ball 0.48 s later at x = -12.8 (7 m from the keeper's goal line): 40/40 turnovers, 1 goal.
  3. ON, the keeper waits (the covered receiver gives chance < 0.75), the rival closes in, and he clears when the rival is within 5 m (`DistributionRushRadius`) or patience is over: mean 3.4 m from the ball at release, first Away touch 0.13 s later at x = -15.8 (4 m from the goal line): 40/40 turnovers, 17 goals (release to goal 0.85 s on average).
  4. S3 (rival at 3 m, identical in both configs): 40/40 turnovers at x = -16.9, 0.03 s after the release; 3/40 goals. So the turnover position alone does not explain the goal rate between S3 and S4-on (a save in S3 seed 2, the shot angle, the keeper's position): PENDING.
- Not attributable to: the Away keeper (no goals for Home at all, he is never involved), an outfield error, or random loss of possession (the turnover is 100 % in both configs). Attributable to the combination: wait until the rival is close + a clearance that only goes 12 m. The first part is a Stage 1 decision (the hurry test is a distance threshold, 5 m, applied when the rival is already almost on him); the second is pre-existing.
- Proposal (needs your authorization, not done): (a) make the hurry test predictive: time for the nearest rival to reach him (distance / his speed) against the time the keeper needs to play, instead of a fixed 5 m; and/or (b) make the targetless kick a real clearance (landing well beyond the nearest rival's reach and wide of the middle). Test: the S4 paired matrix (goals against on <= off + noise) without worsening S2 (retained 40/40 on vs 21/40 off) or S1.

## S6 — Novice vs Elite: CAUSE CONFIRMED
- Traces (seed 1, on): Novice: interval 0.23 s, perception 0.25 s, skill decision making 0.32, patience 1.48 s. Elite: interval 0.10 s, perception 0.11 s, decision making 0.95, patience 2.43 s. In both the covered-receiver scene has the nearest rival to the keeper going 10.7 m -> 5.7 m (1.38 s) -> 4.99 m (1.48 s): the rival crosses the 5 m hurry radius at about 1.45 s. Elite releases at the first due frame after that (1.483 s). Novice's patience (1.48 s) and the hurry radius coincide at about 1.45 s and his due frames are every 0.23 s: the next is 1.617 s.
- So the release instants (1.62 vs 1.56 s; Elite 1.483-1.683 s over 40 seeds, Novice 1.617 in all 40) are set by when the rival arrives and by each level's decision interval, not by patience: Elite's own patience (2.43 s) is never reached.
- Design: patience is `DistributionPatienceSeconds x (floor + (1 - floor) x skill.DecisionMaking)`, and `skill.DecisionMaking` is the difficulty's `GoalkeeperParameters.DecisionMaking` (Novice 0.30 ... Elite 0.90) scaled by his Command, via `GoalkeeperSkillModel` (the same difficulty parameter Phase 16 uses for his judgement of exits). So difficulty does affect patience directly by construction (Stage 1 design); S5 shows it when nothing forces the release (Low: 1.33 s = its 1.32 s patience; High: 1.57 s, stopped by the rival). The behaviour is coherent with that design; the S6 scene just cannot isolate patience. No new rule invented.
- OFF, the 0.68 s (Novice) vs 0.18 s (Elite) are the existing Phase 14 perception delay + decision interval of each level.
- Proposal (optional, not done): a scene variant where no rival approaches the keeper (e.g. the Away press disabled for that scene) so S6 measures patience only.

## Bench changes made by this investigation (tests only)
`waitDecisions` -> `waitSecondsBeforeRelease` (first possession only, seconds); new columns `goalBy` and `firstAwayTouchSeconds`. Same assertions (the switched-off keeper never waits; with it on, waits show up).
